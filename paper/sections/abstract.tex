When the sources retrieved for a query disagree, the right response depends on \emph{why} they disagree: a
question with several valid answers, a contested topic, stale information and a false source each call for
different behaviour. \citet{cattan2025dragged} showed that telling a generator the conflict type improves its
responses by about 24 points, but that predicting the type is hard even for frontier LLMs. We study whether
\emph{typed decision models}, which return probabilities over fixed options in a single forward pass instead of
generating text, can do this classification fast and cheaply. Using the \conflicts benchmark and the original
paper's prompt verbatim, we evaluate all \ndm decision models served by one commercial inference gateway, from
\nproviders providers, against Gemini models on identical inputs. Asked directly, the decision models reach
\dmaccmin--\dmaccmax\% accuracy; \natpar of the \ndm match or exceed Gemini~2.5 Flash, the paper's model
(\gtwofiveacc\%), at \costratiorefmin--\costratiorefmax$\times$ lower billed cost (\nfree were free) and
\latratiorefmin--\latratiorefmax$\times$ lower latency. The current Gemini~3.8 Flash (\gthreeeightacc\%) is significantly more accurate than \nworsenow of
them, but sending each decision model's least confident quarter of queries to it gives a cascade statistically tied
with it for \ncasctied of the \ndm, at \casctiedcostmin--\casctiedcostmax\% of its cost. End to end, answers
generated with the types of \eentied of the \ndm follow the expected behaviour as often as with the LLM's types. Decomposing the taxonomy into yes/no questions lowers
accuracy for every model, and a repair fitted on one model does not transfer. Pre-specified diagnostics show that
dates play no measurable role and that sensitivity to source order varies widely between models. Decision models
detect a planted false source less reliably than the LLM as hard labels, but their probabilities still separate
planted from clean sources. We release code, prompts and
all model outputs\releasenote.
