\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluated the typed decision model \jev as a classifier of knowledge-conflict types among retrieved sources,
under the protocol of the benchmark's authors and against LLMs on identical inputs. Asked directly, \jev matches the
accuracy of the LLM used in the original study at a fraction of its cost and latency, and it is the fastest system
we tested. It trails the current LLM, but a confidence-gated cascade recovers most of that gap at under a third of
the LLM's cost. Decomposing the task into yes/no questions does not work reliably, and pre-specified diagnostics
show that \jev's known weaknesses with dates and context do not translate into specific failures on this task.
Future work should test whether \jev's predicted types, and its probabilities over them, improve the answers of
search-augmented systems end to end, and whether its confidence can steer a generator between expected behaviours
when the type is uncertain.

\section*{Ethics and Broader Impact}

The planted-misinformation variant rewrites one retrieved page per query to state a wrong answer. It is built from
public data, contains only factual substitutions on non-sensitive questions, and is released with clear labels for
research on detection. All queries in \conflicts are safe by construction \citep{cattan2025dragged}. Faster and
cheaper conflict classification could be used to suppress minority viewpoints if \emph{Conflicting opinions} were
treated as an error; the taxonomy's expected behaviour for that type is to present all viewpoints.
