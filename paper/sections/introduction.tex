\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Search-augmented language models answer from retrieved web pages, and those pages often disagree. The
disagreement can have very different causes. A question may be underspecified and admit several correct answers;
it may touch a contested topic on which reasonable sources differ; the answer may have changed over time, so some
pages are stale; or a page may simply be wrong. Each case calls for a different response: combine the answers,
present the viewpoints neutrally, prefer the most recent information, or ignore the unreliable source.
\citet{cattan2025dragged} formalised this as a five-way taxonomy, released \conflicts, a benchmark of 458 search
queries annotated by experts with their conflict type, and showed that giving a generator the gold type raises
the share of responses with the expected behaviour by about 24 points. The bottleneck is the type itself: the best
LLM they tested, Gemini~2.5 Flash, predicted it with 65.3\% accuracy.

Conflict typing is a decision, not a generation task. It is made once per query, before the answer is written, and
its output is one of five labels. A classifier for it should therefore be fast enough to sit in front of every
search-augmented request, cheap enough to run on all of them, and calibrated enough that its uncertainty can be
acted on. These are exactly the properties claimed for a new class of \emph{typed decision models}, which take
unstructured context and typed questions and return probabilities over the allowed answers in a single forward
pass, without generating text \citep{typesafe2026jev}. \jev, the first such model, has been evaluated broadly on
classification, NLI and judging benchmarks \citep{deusser2026jev,li2026specialized,duan2026general}, but never on
deciding why retrieved sources conflict, a task that requires reading many documents at once and reasoning about
their mutual relation rather than about one text.

We ask whether \jev can serve as this decision layer. We make four contributions.

\paragraph{(1) A faithful evaluation of \jev on conflict typing.} We evaluate \jev on \conflicts under the
original paper's protocol: the same input representation, the same category definitions and examples taken
verbatim from the paper's source, and Gemini rerun on identical inputs so that every comparison is paired. Asked
directly, \jev is statistically tied with the paper's model, Gemini~2.5 Flash (\jevacc\% vs.\ \gtwofiveacc\%), at
\costratiotwofive$\times$ lower cost and \latratiotwofive$\times$ lower latency (\S\ref{sec:main}). It trails the
current Gemini~3.8 Flash by \jevvsthreeeightdiffabs points, and among five decision models it is mid-pack in
accuracy and the fastest.

\paragraph{(2) A formulation study.} Decision models invite decomposing a taxonomy into simple yes/no questions.
We find that this fails for conflict typing (\treeoneacc\%) because \jev answers \emph{yes} too readily; a
reordered tree chosen on development data repairs it for \jev but does not transfer to other decision models,
leaving the direct question as the robust format (\S\ref{sec:formulation}).

\paragraph{(3) A cost-quality cascade.} Because \jev's confidence predicts its own errors, sending its least
certain queries to an LLM is effective: escalating a quarter of the queries to Gemini~3.8 Flash reaches
\cascflattfacc\% against the LLM's \gthreeeightacc\% ($p\cascflattfp$) at \cascflattfcost\% of the LLM's cost
(\S\ref{sec:cascade}).

\paragraph{(4) Pre-specified diagnostics.} We test three hypotheses about how \jev reads conflicting sources
(\S\ref{sec:diagnostics}). \jev's documented weakness at ordering dates \citep{typesafe2026jagged} does not affect
this task: no system's accuracy changes when dates are removed or when recency is computed in code, and \jev
recognises outdated sources from their content as well as the LLM does. \jev is as sensitive to source order as
the LLM, and when one source is rewritten to contradict the others, it separates the corrupted from the clean
version of a query exactly as well as Gemini~3.8 Flash.

Every setting was fixed on a development split before the test runs, and every later addition (a newer LLM, a
newer \jev version, other decision models, the diagnostics) was specified in writing before it was run. We release
the code, prompts, splits and all model outputs\releasenote.
