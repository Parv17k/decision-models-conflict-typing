\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What \jev offers as a conflict classifier.} Conflict typing sits on the critical path of every
search-augmented answer, so its latency and cost add to every request. \jev classifies a query with about ten
retrieved pages in \jevlat\,s for \$\jevcost per thousand queries, with the accuracy of the LLM used in the
original study. That makes conflict typing affordable as an always-on step. When the accuracy of a
current LLM is required, \jev's confidence is good enough to route only its uncertain queries to the LLM, which
recovers most of the gap at under a third of the LLM's cost. The probabilities have a second use: a system that
receives a distribution over conflict types can hedge between expected behaviours when two types are close, which
a hard label does not allow.

\paragraph{How to ask.} The direct choice question was the most robust format. The yes/no decomposition looked
natural but inherited \jev's yes-bias; the repaired tree is better calibrated but encodes choices fitted to \jev on
a small development split, and it did not carry over to four other decision models. For a new decision model we
would start from the flat question.

\paragraph{What the diagnostics change.} Two widely reported weaknesses of decision models predicted specific
failures here, and neither appeared. Date handling, a documented weakness of \jev, turned out to be irrelevant
because outdated sources are recognised from their content. Order sensitivity is real, but the LLM shares it. The
planted-source test shows that one contradicting page out of about ten is noticed as reliably by \jev as by the LLM,
at the cost of more false alarms that a threshold can control. Conflict typing therefore does not inherit these
weaknesses in the way one might fear; it does inherit the general limit that decision models are weaker on long
inputs \citep{duan2026general}, and our inputs are long.

\paragraph{\jev among decision models.} \jev is not the most accurate decision model on this task. Perplexity's
decider is significantly more accurate and, with the tree question, ties the current LLM. \jev's advantages are
speed, the strongest \emph{Outdated} recall, and well-behaved confidence for escalation. Since every decision model
accepted \jev's questions unchanged, practitioners can swap models without changing the pipeline.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(i) \conflicts is small: with \ntest test queries, differences under about five points are not significant, and the
cascade can be shown close to, but not formally equivalent to, the LLM. (ii) The label distribution reflects how the
queries were selected, not real search traffic, and the dataset has only five natural \emph{Misinformation}
queries; our planted variant tests one kind of misinformation (an entity or value substituted in a single source).
(iii) Hosted models change without notice; three of the decision models have unversioned identifiers, and our rerun
of Gemini~2.5 Flash scores below the published figure. We release all outputs so that results can be checked without
rerunning them. (iv) We evaluate classification only; we do not measure whether a predicted type improves the final
answer, which \citet{cattan2025dragged} established for the gold type. (v) Neither the decision models nor the LLMs
were deterministic: identical requests changed 2--3\% of predictions.
