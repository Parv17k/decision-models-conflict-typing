\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Inputs.} Every system sees the same input. As in \citet{cattan2025dragged}, each search result is the
concatenation of its URL, title, snippet, publication date (when available) and 512-token segment; results keep the
dataset's order and are numbered. For the decision models they form the state; for the LLMs they fill the paper's
prompt.

\paragraph{Decision models.} We run every model that the Vercel AI Gateway\footnote{\url{https://vercel.com/ai-gateway}} listed as a
decision model on the day of the census (25 October 2026),
with a pinned identifier where one exists: \jev~1.13.0 and 1.14.0 \citep{typesafe2026jev}, Decider v1.2 and Decider Flash v1.2
\citep{perplexity2026decider,perplexity2026deciderflash}, GPT-6 Luna Decisions \citep{openai2026decisions}, d1 and d1
Ultra \citep{liquid2026d1,liquid2026d1ultra}, Decision-1 \citep{microsoft2026decision}, Elephant-1 and Mammoth-1
\citep{baseten2026decision}, and Classify-1 \citep{scaledown2026classify}. All receive the questions of \S\ref{sec:method} unchanged.
Classify-1's API accepts only a single text field, so it receives the same content as one text, the numbered
results exactly as the LLMs see them. Two entries were excluded before any run. Laya, the only open decision model
served by the gateway, cannot accept the task's question, whose category definitions exceed its input limit; prior
work reports it near chance without task-specific training \citep{duan2026general,nandakishore2026laya}.
\texttt{typesafe-ai/jev} is a floating alias of a pinned version we already run. Open-weight models that the gateway
does not serve are out of scope: their cost and latency would depend on local hardware and not be comparable.

\paragraph{LLM baselines.} We rerun the paper's best classifier, Gemini~2.5 Flash \citep{comanici2025gemini}, with the paper's prompt
verbatim, temperature 0, thinking off (the paper lists Gemini 2.5 Flash and its thinking variant separately) and,
as a sensitivity check, thinking on. To compare against a current model we add Gemini~3.8 Flash, the newest Flash
model available to us, at its lowest thinking level (it cannot be switched off) and at its default. The prompt asks
for JSON with an explanation and a category number; answers stating the category in another format (\nlenient of
\ngemini) are parsed leniently rather than counted as errors. All models are reached through this gateway, which reports the billed cost of every request (prices may
differ from the providers' own APIs); latency is the wall-clock time of the successful request.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Prompts, questions, inputs and the development/test split were frozen before any test-set
call, with file hashes recorded. The only setting chosen by inspecting data is tree v2, chosen on the development
split and marked $\dagger$ in all tables. Every later addition (Gemini~3.8 Flash, the further decision models, the
cascade, the diagnostics and the end-to-end test) was specified in writing before it was run, and every run is reported
(Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{Statistics.} Comparisons are paired on identical queries. We report 95\% bootstrap intervals over queries
\citep{efron1994bootstrap} (10,000 resamples) and two-sided exact McNemar tests \citep{mcnemar1947} on per-query
correctness. With \ntest test queries, a 95\% interval on accuracy is about $\pm$\cihalf points wide, so differences
under about five points are generally not significant; we report them as ties. We do not correct for multiple
comparisons; with \ndm models a few nominal differences at $p<0.05$ are expected by chance, so we draw conclusions
from patterns across models rather than from single tests.
